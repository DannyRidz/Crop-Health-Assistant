# J2D demo narration

Working recording script, prepared 16 October 2026. No video has been recorded or uploaded. Read the quoted passages aloud; directions and headings are not narration. Either team member can narrate.

The organiser confirmed the complete video must be **strictly under three minutes**. Target **2:45 total**, including credits. The timeline below totals 165 seconds; it is an editing and rehearsal plan, not a measured recording. Keep the actual export below 180 seconds. The previous optional extension is removed. Record genuine app behaviour; shorten processing waits transparently instead of rushing the narration.

## 1. Start with the product, 0:00 to 0:18

Show https://coffee-leaf-check.web.app with the four steps visible. Use the app rather than a long title animation.

> A coffee farmer sees spots on a leaf. What should they check next? We are team J2D. Crop Health Assistant turns coffee leaf photos into useful findings and next steps. It is accessible worldwide, with PNG users as our first planned validation group.

## 2. Add the photo and answers, 0:18 to 0:48

Upload one permitted BRACOL whole leaf photo. Show the permission checkbox and choose Check photos. Answer the four questions. For dataset history that is unavailable, choose Not sure. Select Brazil because that is the image source country.

> The farmer adds a leaf photo and gives permission for analysis. Four short questions add context, with Not sure options. This example uses a licensed BRACOL image from Brazil. We select Brazil and leave unknown growing history as Not sure. Country is optional; no exact location is needed.

During the actual request, show a short section of the existing architecture slide. Keep the original request recording for continuity. If editing out a wait, add “Processing wait shortened.”

## 3. Explain the result, 0:48 to 1:30

Show the actual returned findings, one possible cause, its supporting evidence and next check. Read the output before narration. Do not promise that a particular disease will appear.

> The result shows visible changes, possible causes, why each may fit and what needs checking. The next check helps the farmer gather useful evidence. Suggestions are based on the photos and answers, rather than a confirmed diagnosis. Published care sources match the selected country: PNG, Indonesia, Kenya or Brazil. When suitable regional guidance is missing, the app explains that gap. Chemical products and doses are not currently available.

If no possible cause is returned, show the real outcome and say: “For this photo, the app could describe visible signs but could not support a possible cause.” Replace the first two sentences accordingly. Never overlay an invented result.

## 4. Show language and report, 1:30 to 2:00

Switch to Tok Pisin, briefly show the same case, then prepare and download the PDF. Show its summary, photo findings and one care/source section. Keep the same case reference and country.

> The same check can be read in English or Tok Pisin. Tok Pisin wording, including the latest edits, and sample reports have completed language review. New case text is automatically translated. The farmer can download a PDF containing the photos, findings, care sources and limitations.

## 5. Explain the build and evidence, 2:00 to 2:35

Show the architecture slide, then return to the app. No cloud console or credential screen is needed.

> Firebase Hosting provides the public address. Cloud Run runs our Next.js server and calls Gemini through the official Google SDK. Secret Manager holds credentials. Cases are temporary and PDFs stay on the device, so this MVP needs no Firestore database. Real Gemini journeys, both PDF languages and 127 software tests have been verified. Agricultural evaluation and farmer testing are next.

## 6. Close, 2:35 to 2:45

Show the app address and team credits. Let credits remain readable while speaking.

> Crop Health Assistant gives coffee farmers a clearer starting point: visible evidence, possible causes and practical next checks. Thank you.
